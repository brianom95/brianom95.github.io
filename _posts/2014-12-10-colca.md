8,000 years ago, the Cabanas & Collaguas settled in a deep canyon that had a reliable river and shade from its cliffs. They built irrigation systems and agricultural terraces that are still used today. 600 years ago, the Incan Empire added more advanced farms and food storage warehouses, known as *colcas*. 400 years ago, the Spanish exploited local labour, leading to small colonial towns and churches. Now free, the people of the canyon are proud of their preserved traditions in agricultural and textiles.

The Colca Canyon as it's now known, is one of deepest in the world. It has a depth of 4,160m which is double that of the Grand Canyon. It's also the best place to see the largest flying bird in the world, the Andean Condor. These have a wingspan of over three meters and are incredible to see up close. The terraces and vibrant cultures deep below make this a popular destination for tourists, either to look down from viewpoints on a bus trip, or to brave the hills and head down!
