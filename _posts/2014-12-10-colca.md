8,000 years ago, the Cabanas & Collaguas settled in a deep canyon that had a reliable river and shade from its cliffs. They built irrigation systems and agricultural terraces that are still used today. 600 years ago, the Incan Empire added more advanced farms and food storage warehouses, known as *colcas*. 400 years ago, the Spanish exploited local labour, leading to small towns and churches. Now free from colonial rule, the local people are proud of their preserved traditions in agricultural and textiles.

The Colca Canyon has since become famous for being one of deepest in the world. It has a depth of 4,160m which is double that of the Grand Canyon. It's also the best place to see the largest flying bird in the world, the Andean Condor. These have a wingspan of over three meters and are incredible to see up close. The terraces and vibrant cultures deep below make this a popular destination for tourists, either to look in from viewpoints on a bus trip, or to brave the hills and head down!

The hike always starts in Cabanaconde, and is usally 3-days. Guided trips do the overcrowded east loop; sleeping in San Juan de Chucco & Sangalle-Oasis. The west loop is more authentic, staying in Llahuar & Sangalle-Oasis. Both share the same last day, walking directly up a 1,000m cliff back to Cabanaconde. This hike is perfect for hiking independently, and Kevin made it his first thru-hike doing the 3-day west loop solo.

I was feeling very fit and wanted a challenge. I decided to trail run both loops, stopping only in Sangalle-Oasis on the same night as Kevin. The day he started his hike I climbed [Chachani (6,075m)](chachani), then still tired I took a 2:40am bus to Cabanaconde, which had me starting the at 9:00am.
