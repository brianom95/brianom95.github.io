From Caribbean cities to Antarctica, this series follows my backpacking journey through Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay and Brazil. This is Part II: Backpacking Peru & Bolivia.

---

## Peru

#### Huaraz

Huaraz sits high in the Cordillera Blanca and is considered one of the best trekking bases in South America. Surrounded by snow-capped peaks, glaciers and turquoise lakes, it attracts hikers from around the world looking to explore Peru beyond Machu Picchu.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa11.jpeg">
</p>

#### Lima

Lima is Peru's sprawling capital and home to nearly a third of the country's population. Founded by the Spanish in 1535, it combines colonial history with a modern Pacific coastline and has developed a reputation as one of the best food cities in the world. Many travellers pass through quickly, but there's plenty to explore.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa12.jpeg">
</p>

#### Huacachina

Huacachina is a tiny oasis town surrounded by huge sand dunes in the Peruvian desert. Once a retreat for wealthy Peruvians, it's now a backpacker favourite for sandboarding, dune buggies and watching sunsets over an otherwise empty landscape.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa13.jpeg">
</p>

#### Arequipa

Arequipa is known as the White City thanks to its buildings made from volcanic stone. Overlooked by towering volcanoes, it offers colonial architecture, excellent food and access to the nearby Colca Canyon, making it one of Peru's most popular stops.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa14.jpeg">
</p>

#### Lake Titicaca

Lake Titicaca sits high in the Andes on the border of Peru and Bolivia and is considered the birthplace of the Inca civilisation. The world's highest navigable lake is home to traditional communities and floating reed islands that have preserved ways of life stretching back centuries.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa15.jpeg">
</p>

#### Cusco

Cusco was once the capital of the Inca Empire and remains one of South America's great travel hubs. Its cobbled streets, Spanish churches and Inca foundations reflect centuries of history, while its altitude and surrounding mountains make it the gateway to the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa16.jpeg">
</p>

#### Machu Picchu

Hidden among cloud-covered mountains, Machu Picchu is the most famous remnant of the Inca Empire and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. Built in the fifteenth century and abandoned before the Spanish conquest, it remained largely unknown to the outside world until its rediscovery in 1911.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa17.jpeg">
</p>

## Bolivia

#### Copacabana

Copacabana is a quiet lakeside town on the shores of Lake Titicaca and serves as Bolivia's gateway to Isla del Sol. According to Inca legend, the island was where the sun itself was born. The relaxed atmosphere makes it a pleasant first stop after crossing from Peru.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa18.jpeg">
</p>

#### La Paz

La Paz is the world's highest capital city and one of South America's most dramatic urban settings, spread across a steep valley beneath the snow-covered Illimani mountain. Cable cars crisscross the city above busy markets and colonial streets, while the surrounding mountains attract hikers and cyclists.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa19.jpeg">
</p>

#### Cochabamba & Torotoro

Cochabamba sits in a fertile valley and enjoys one of Bolivia's mildest climates, earning it the nickname "City of Eternal Spring". Nearby Torotoro National Park remains one of the country's lesser-visited highlights, known for dinosaur footprints, canyons and extensive cave systems.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa20.jpeg">
</p>

#### Sucre

Sucre is Bolivia's constitutional capital and one of the most beautiful colonial cities in South America. Its whitewashed buildings and relaxed pace make it popular with backpackers, particularly those taking Spanish classes or looking to escape the altitude of La Paz.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa21.jpeg">
</p>

#### Potosí Mines

Potosí was once one of the richest cities in the world thanks to the vast silver deposits of Cerro Rico. The wealth extracted here financed much of the Spanish Empire, though it came at an enormous human cost. Today visitors can descend into the still-operating mines and learn about one of history's greatest resource booms.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa22.jpeg">
</p>

#### Uyuni Salt Flats

The Salar de Uyuni is the largest salt flat on Earth, formed from prehistoric lakes that evaporated thousands of years ago. Stretching across southwest Bolivia, the region also contains colourful lagoons, volcanoes and strange rock formations, making it one of the continent's most surreal landscapes.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa23.jpeg">
</p>

From Bolivia I crossed into Chile, beginning the long journey south through Patagonia and eventually towards Antarctica itself.
