My first marathon was the 2021 [Killarney Ironman](killarney-full-ironman), which gave me the confidence to take on harder events, and a need for a new goal. I had plans to travel when pandemic restrictions eased, so I looked for something that would work well that. I researched events in extreme locations like Antartica, and found the Seven Continents Club: the small group of people who have run a marathon on every continent. At the time more people had been to space, and ten times more people had summitted Everest, than had done this.

The Antarctica Marathon began in 1995, sailing south on Soviet oil tankers to run a tough course along research station supply trails. Nowadays the event is more official, using expedition cruise ships and doing more while there. There's an award ceremony on the ship for those completing their final continent, so I knew Antarctica should be my final one. The race had a three year waiting list, so I booked it and started to plan which races I would do.

## 1. Europe
#### Rome, Italy
**27 March 2022**  
**04:23:53**

My first official marathon, and an introduction to big city races. Rome felt chaotic due to the huge crowds, cobbled streets, pacers, music and sun. I started too fast and held on longer than I should have, before the heat slowly broke me down. The final 10km felt like a fight for survival, relying on aid stations and any shade I could find. It wasn’t pretty, but it was a neccessary introduction to what marathon running actually demands. It took 30 minutes in the shade to feel okay after, which made the road ahead look pretty terrifying.

## 2. North America
#### Vancouver, Canada
**01 May 2022**  
**04:27:18**

Vancouver is my favourite race. A clean, fast course through the city and along the seawall. I found a nice rhythm with my friend Conall for most of the race, but I did drift in the second half as I again struggled with heat. I loved seeing huge bridges and skyscrapers, plus the support on the route is incredible, but it didn't fully distract from what was again a fight to finish. I have lots of friends in Vancouver so this trip was a long overdue catchup with them. Great memories that only exist because of this goal.

## 3. Oceania
#### Auckland, New Zealand
**30 October 2022**  
**04:45:27**

Auckland was also defined by weather but this time wind, rain and flooded roads. A huge storm threatened the start by delaying ferries, but I made it to the line where I awaited the countdown in ankle deep water. I spent two months in New Zealand and was constantly blown away by epic landscapes. This race day had none of that. Just an absolute slog with soggy shoes, enjoying any moment where the clouds parted and rains softened. A lesson in self motivation and chafing that I'm proud to have powered through.

Read my full article [here](auckland-marathon).

## 4. Asia
#### Chiang Mai, Thailand
**18 December 2022**  
**04:56:49**

A night marathon through Chiang Mai’s ancient city walls, designed to avoid the heat but still brutally humid. The start along the city walls and moat were unreal, lit with the glow of dim red lights. Outside the city we ran along rivers and long dark stretches where staying awake was part of the challenge. Pineapple, sunrise and steady pacing slowly brought me back into it, finishing as the city woke up around me.

## 5. Africa
#### Marrakech, Morocco
**29 June 2023**  
**04:45:26**

Marrakech was a mix of family, travel and racing through one of the busiest cities in Africa. I ran much of it with my dad, which made the early kilometres fly by, before the race spread out into the outskirts of the city and desert edges. The weather was perfect, but the race still turned into a grind in places, finished with a final push back towards the medina.

## 6. South America
#### Salento, Colombia
**03 November 2024**  
**07:40:25**

Easily the toughest marathon of the seven. Salento was more mountain expedition than road race, with over 3,000m of climbing, river crossings, and trails that barely resembled paths at all. Mud, altitude and steep descents made running almost secondary at times. It was brutal from the first kilometre, but the coffee region and cloud forest scenery made it unforgettable.

## 7. Antarctica
#### King George Island
**25 March 2025**  
**05:38:22**

The final continent and the reason the whole project had structure from the beginning. A three-year waiting list led to a slow build of races across the world, all timed to end here. Getting to Antarctica meant sailing the Drake Passage, waiting for weather windows, and hoping the marathon would even go ahead.

The race itself was unlike anything else: six laps between research stations, across mud, rock, sand and wind that never really stopped. You passed penguins, ice-cold water, and landscapes that felt completely detached from anywhere else on Earth. The finish line felt less like the end of a race and more like the closing of something much longer.

Read my full article [here](antarctica).
