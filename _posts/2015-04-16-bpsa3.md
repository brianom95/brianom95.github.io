From Caribbean cities to Antarctica, this series follows my backpacking journey through Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile, Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay and Brazil. This is Part III: Backpacking Chile, Argentina & Antarctica.

---

## Chile

#### San Pedro de Atacama

San Pedro de Atacama is a desert oasis surrounded by salt flats, volcanoes and landscapes that resemble another planet. Sitting in one of the driest places on Earth, it has become northern Chile's main backpacker hub, with excursions to geysers, lagoons and some of the clearest night skies anywhere.

</p>

#### Valparaíso

Valparaíso is a chaotic port city spread across steep hills overlooking the Pacific. Once one of South America's wealthiest ports, it declined after the Panama Canal opened, but its colourful houses, street art and bohemian atmosphere have made it one of Chile's most distinctive destinations.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa25.jpeg">
</p>

#### Santiago

Santiago is Chile's modern capital, sitting beneath the snow-capped Andes. Home to nearly half the country's population, it blends skyscrapers with colonial buildings and serves as the gateway to much of central Chile. Most travellers pass through on their way to Patagonia or neighbouring Argentina.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa26.jpeg">
</p>

#### Aconcagua

*incl. Mendoza & San Juan*

Aconcagua rises to 6,961 metres, making it the highest mountain outside Asia. The region around Mendoza and San Juan is famous for wine, dry mountain landscapes and easy access to the Andes. For climbers and trekkers, it marks one of South America's great adventures.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa27.jpeg">
</p>

#### Puerto Natales

Puerto Natales sits on the edge of Chilean Patagonia and serves as the gateway to Torres del Paine National Park. Originally founded by sheep farmers and fishermen, it has become one of the world's great trekking bases, attracting travellers heading into Patagonia's mountains and glaciers.

    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa28.jpeg">
</p>

## Argentina

#### El Calafate

El Calafate is a windswept Patagonian town best known for the nearby Perito Moreno Glacier. Once little more than a sheep farming settlement, it now serves as the main base for exploring southern Patagonia and one of the few places where visitors can easily stand beside a constantly advancing glacier.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa29.jpeg">
</p>

#### El Chaltén

El Chaltén was founded in 1985 to strengthen Argentina's claim over Patagonia, but it quickly became known as the trekking capital of the country. Surrounded by jagged peaks and glaciers, nearly every trail begins directly from town, making it a paradise for hikers.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa30.jpeg">
</p>

#### Ushuaia

Ushuaia claims to be the southernmost city in the world and sits between the Beagle Channel and the mountains of Tierra del Fuego. Once a penal colony, it now attracts travellers looking to explore Patagonia, sail the channel, or continue further south to Antarctica.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa31.jpeg">
</p>

#### Antarctica

Antarctica is the coldest, driest and windiest continent on Earth, protected by international treaty and inhabited only by scientists and temporary visitors. Expeditions from Ushuaia cross the Drake Passage to reach a landscape of glaciers, icebergs and wildlife unlike anywhere else on the planet.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa32.jpeg">
</p>

#### Buenos Aires

Buenos Aires is often called the Paris of South America, though its personality is distinctly Argentine. Built on wealth generated from cattle and grain exports, the city combines grand architecture with neighbourhoods famous for football, tango and steak. It remains one of the continent's great capitals.

<p align="center" width="100%">
    <img width="400" src="/images/bpsa33.jpeg">
</p>

Leaving Patagonia behind, I turned north once more. The final stretch would take me through the quieter countries of Uruguay and Paraguay before ending among the beaches and giant cities of Brazil.
